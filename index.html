The Problem

Why the EU decided to act: three structural failures in today's data economy

80%

Wasted Data

The vast majority of industrial data generated by connected devices is never used. An enormous economic potential is lost every single day.

🔒

Excluded Users

Those who purchase a connected product cannot access the data it generates. Manufacturers control everything — even data that rightfully belongs to the user.

🏢

Data Monopoly

Big tech companies dominate data access, creating competitive imbalances that harm SMEs and consumers alike.

⚖️

Abusive Contracts

Small businesses suffer clauses imposed by more powerful players: unilateral contract changes, very short notice periods, and prohibitions on legal recourse.

☁️

Cloud Lock-in

Switching cloud providers is costly and complex. Companies remain trapped with a single vendor even when better alternatives exist.

📊

Unbalanced Market

Without clear rules, whoever holds the data dictates the terms. The Data Act was created to build a fair, competitive market for everyone.

Who & What It Covers

The Data Act applies to all companies placing connected products on the EU market, regardless of where they are headquartered

Key Actors

👤 The Three Roles

  • Data holder — the manufacturer or service provider that collects the data. Must make it accessible to users in structured, machine-readable formats.
  • Data user — the natural or legal person who owns or has contractual use of the connected product. Includes both consumers and businesses.
  • Third parties — entities chosen by the user with whom data can be shared (e.g. alternative service providers, independent analysts, apps).
Included

✅ What Falls Under the Act

  • Raw data collected directly from sensors or device components.
  • Pre-processed data elaborated by the device before transmission.
  • Data from any connected product: cars, smart appliances, industrial machinery, medical devices, wearables.
  • All companies placing connected products in the EU market, regardless of where they are based.
Excluded

❌ What Is Excluded

  • Micro and small enterprises (with some specific exceptions).
  • Derived data resulting from the manufacturer's own processing.
  • Content protected by intellectual property rights.
  • Data used for law enforcement, customs, or tax purposes.
The 5 Pillars

The five fundamental principles of EU Regulation 2023/2854, explained in full detail

01

🔓 User Access to Data

  • Anyone who owns or uses a connected product has the right to access the data it generates — for free, easily, and in a usable format.
  • The user can share that data with any third party of their choice (e.g. independent repair shops, competing apps, alternative energy providers).
  • Recipients may only use the data for agreed purposes: they cannot use it to develop competing products or to train AI models that compete with the original data holder.
  • Data must be delivered in structured, commonly used, and machine-readable formats.
Example: your connected car collects maintenance data → you can share it with any mechanic, not just the official dealer.

Source: European Commission / EUR-Lex Regulation text

02

🤝 Fair B2B Data Sharing

  • SMEs are protected from abusive clauses imposed by larger companies in data-sharing contracts.
  • Banned practices include: unilateral contract changes, prohibition of legal recourse, and very short notice periods.
  • The Commission has developed non-binding Model Contract Terms to help businesses negotiate on equal terms.
  • Fair remuneration is foreseen for the data holder that makes data available to others.
  • Member States may establish certified dispute resolution bodies for cases where the parties cannot reach agreement.

Source: European Commission / Agenda Digitale (September 2025)

03

☁️ Cloud Freedom & Switching

  • End of vendor lock-in through a phased roadmap fully grounded in the regulation.
  • From 12 September 2025: complete transparency on all switching and migration costs.
  • From 12 January 2026: progressive reduction of exit fees between cloud providers.
  • From 12 January 2027: total elimination of switching fees — changing provider is completely free.
  • Data must be exportable in standard formats; providers are legally required to assist with migration.
  • Rules apply across all cloud layers: IaaS, PaaS, and SaaS.

Source: Osservatori Digital Innovation, Politecnico di Milano (January 2026)

04

🏛️ Public Access in Emergencies

  • Public authorities, the EU Commission, the ECB, and EU bodies may request private sector data in exceptional circumstances (e.g. floods, wildfires, public health crises).
  • Access must be proportionate, strictly time-limited, and compliant with data protection law.
  • Excessive fees cannot be charged for emergency data requests.
  • This power does not extend to law enforcement, customs, or tax-related purposes.

Source: European Parliament press release, November 2023 / Regulation text Art. 14–22

05

🔒 Protection of Trade Secrets

  • The Data Act explicitly balances data openness with the protection of proprietary business information.
  • Companies are not required to disclose patented technologies, strategic know-how, or commercially sensitive processes.
  • The data holder may adopt technical protection measures to preserve confidentiality of trade secrets during data sharing.
  • This safeguard ensures that greater data access does not come at the cost of legitimate competitive advantages.

Source: EUR-Lex / LCA Studio Legale (September 2025)

In Real Life

Concrete before-and-after scenarios across different sectors

🚗

Connected Car

Before

Maintenance data is only accessible to the official dealership. You are forced to use their services.

After

You can share the data with any independent mechanic. More choice, lower prices.

🏠

Smart Home

Before

Your home's energy consumption data remains locked by the smart meter manufacturer.

After

Share your consumption data with an alternative energy provider to get better personalised tariffs.

🏭

Smart Factory

Before

Industrial machinery data is controlled solely by the manufacturer. Maintenance must go through them.

After

The factory owner can choose any partner for maintenance, analysis, or process optimisation.

🏥

Medical Device

Before

Data from a cardiac monitor or medical wearable remains the exclusive property of the device manufacturer.

After

The patient can share their clinical data with doctors, specialists, or second-opinion platforms.

🌾

Precision Agriculture

Before

Weather, humidity, and GPS data collected by agricultural machinery stays locked with the tractor supplier.

After

The farmer can use it on any precision farming platform to optimise harvests and reduce waste.

Cloud Migration

Before

Switching cloud providers costs thousands of euros and months of technical work. You're stuck with one vendor.

After

From 2027 the switch is completely free. Data exports in standard formats; the provider must assist with migration.

GDPR vs Data Act

Two regulations that complement — not replace — each other. The GDPR always prevails for personal data or mixed datasets.

GDPR (2018)

  • Protects personal data and individuals' privacy
  • Defensive in nature: limits and regulates data processing
  • Protects natural persons as data subjects
  • Applies to personal data and mixed datasets — always prevails
  • Fines up to 4% of global annual turnover

Data Act (2025)

  • Governs access to all data generated by connected products
  • Proactive in nature: unlocks the data economy
  • Protects consumers, businesses (SMEs), and public authorities
  • Focused on IoT, industrial, and non-personal data
  • Penalties defined by each individual Member State

Timeline

Key milestones of EU Regulation 2023/2854 from proposal to full applicability

23 February 2022

European Commission Proposal

The Commission presents the draft regulation as a cornerstone of the European data strategy.

9 November 2023

European Parliament Approval

481 votes in favour, 31 against, 71 abstentions. The EU Council gives final approval on 27 November.

22 December 2023

Publication in the EU Official Journal

Regulation (EU) 2023/2854 is officially published and numbered.

11 January 2024

Entry into Force

The Data Act formally enters into force within the European legal order.

12 September 2025

✓ Full Applicability — All Main Obligations Binding

Businesses must be compliant. Italy had not yet designated its national competent authority at this date, potentially exposing it to EU infringement proceedings.

12 January 2026

Cloud Fee Reduction Begins

Start of the gradual reduction of switching and exit fees between cloud providers.

12 September 2026

Mandatory Design by Default

New connected products placed on the market must be natively designed to enable data sharing.

12 January 2027

Free Cloud Switching

Complete elimination of exit fees from cloud providers. Maximum portability guaranteed across IaaS, PaaS, and SaaS.
